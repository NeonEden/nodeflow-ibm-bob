NodeFlow starts with a simple frustration. An unclear request costs you three times: you build the wrong thing, the review turns into opinion, and the bug shows up late.

So we changed the order. Every request is written down first, as a contract: what it does, its exact signature, and the cases it has to pass. And the contract is committed before any code exists.

Then IBM Bob 2.0 implements against that text.

Bob built the core of our voice path: the segmenter that decides, while you speak, whether what you just said is a new idea, a correction, or noise. It shipped. We reviewed it, found a real defect, and fixed it before merging.

And here is the part we care about most. The canvas is where that method becomes a product. You speak, the nodes appear, and the request gets written down while it is still fresh.

NodeFlow. Speak the request. Bob builds it against the contract. And nothing is called done until a command on the machine proves it.
